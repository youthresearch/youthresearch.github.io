Research by subject

Every field asks questions differently and counts different things as evidence. Pick your subject for its related areas, ways into a question, methods, sources, and a first-week checklist.

No subjects match that search.

Sciences & math

  • AstronomyStars, planets, and the universe, from telescope data you can access.
  • Biology & lab sciencesLiving things, from cells to ecosystems, through experiments and observation.
  • ChemistryWhat matter is made of and how substances react and change.
  • Computer ScienceAlgorithms, software, and data — building and testing things that compute.
  • EcologyHow organisms interact with each other and their environment.
  • EngineeringDesigning, building, and testing solutions to real problems.
  • Environmental StudiesHow people and the planet affect each other, and what to do about it.
  • MathematicsPatterns, proofs, and problems, where evidence means proof.
  • NeuroscienceThe brain and nervous system, and how they shape behavior.
  • PhysicsMatter, energy, and motion, tested against careful measurement.

Social sciences

  • AnthropologyHuman cultures and communities, studied up close.
  • BusinessHow organizations, markets, and consumers make decisions.
  • CriminologyThe causes of crime and how societies respond to it.
  • Economics & public policyChoices, markets, and policies, measured with real data.
  • EducationHow people learn, and how schools can help them learn better.
  • Gender StudiesHow gender shapes identity, experience, and institutions.
  • International RelationsHow states, institutions, and people interact across borders.
  • LinguisticsHow language is structured, used, and changes over time.
  • Political ScienceGovernment, power, and how people take part in politics.
  • Psychology & social scienceThe mind and behavior, studied with people as participants.
  • Public HealthPatterns of health and illness across populations.
  • SociologyPatterns in how groups, not just individuals, behave.

Humanities & arts

  • ArchitectureBuildings and spaces, and how people use and experience them.
  • Art (studio & practice-based)Making art as research, from process to finished work.
  • Art HistoryArtworks in context — who made them, how, and why.
  • Film StudiesReading films closely for form, story, and context.
  • HistoryThe past, built from primary sources and turned into an argument.
  • Literature & EnglishClose reading of texts to make an argument about meaning.
  • Media StudiesHow media shapes, and is shaped by, the people who use it.
  • MusicThe structure, history, and meaning of music.
  • PhilosophyBig questions about knowledge, ethics, and existence, answered with argument.
